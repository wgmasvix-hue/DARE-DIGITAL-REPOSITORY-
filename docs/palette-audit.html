Colour & contrast audit

The DARE brand hues are unchanged. What changes is where each one is allowed to be used — because three of the four fail WCAG AA at text sizes today, including the primary blue that carries your links.

Standard WCAG 2.1 AA Measured against #FFFFFF Body text needs 4.5:1 Large text & UI needs 3:1

The fix: two tiers per hue

Each brand colour keeps its hue and saturation. Lightness drops only as far as AA requires, producing a display tier for large decorative surfaces and a text tier for anything below 24px. Reaching for a display token on small text is the mistake the naming exists to prevent.

ORCID green is a special case

#a6ce39 is a fixed ORCID brand asset — it can't be darkened where the ORCID mark appears. At 1.82:1 on white it is a background colour only, and the text placed on it must be dark. Most implementations ship white-on-green, which is the worst available combination.

✕ White on green — 1.82:1 Sign in with ORCID
✓ Dark on green — 9.54:1 Sign in with ORCID

Where the word “ORCID” appears as body text rather than on the green, use #667f20 — the same hue at 4.54:1.

Neutrals and boundaries

One rule here is easy to miss: WCAG 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for the visible boundary of an interactive control. Bootstrap's default input border is #adb5bd at 2.07:1, so stock DSpace form fields fail before any theming is applied.

Token Value On white Permitted use

The dark footer needs its own blue

On the #212529 footer, the raw brand blue drops to 4.21:1 and fails. Footer links use #9fb4ff at 7.67:1 instead — this is the usual cause of unreadable DSpace footers.

Ratios computed per WCAG 2.1 relative luminance against #FFFFFF. Swatch test surfaces stay white in dark mode by design — tinting them would misreport the figures.
